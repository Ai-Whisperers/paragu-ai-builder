Every week we talk to a European investor who's already researched Paraguay, has their plan, and asks: "when can we start?" Our honest answer is usually: "you can start Paraguay today. But you can't finish Europe until next spring."

Getting Paraguayan residency is the well-defined half of the emigration. Unwinding your European tax ties is where the friction lives. This post is what the home-country side actually looks like by jurisdiction — enough to plan around, not enough to replace your local tax advisor.

## The universal rule

Paraguayan residency makes you a Paraguayan tax resident. It does **not** automatically make you stop being a tax resident somewhere else. Each European country has its own rules for breaking tax residency — and several of them actively tax the moment of departure.

Three categories of friction, in order of how much they cost investors:

1. **Exit taxes** — charged when you leave (Netherlands, Germany, some French structures)
2. **Continuing obligations** — carry on after you leave (UK deemed domicile, Belgian securities tax)
3. **Filing complexity** — not a cost, but a paperwork burden (Spain, Italy)

## Netherlands — the high-friction case

The Netherlands is the most complicated EU jurisdiction we regularly work with. Three specific items:

**Box 3 on worldwide wealth.** As long as you're NL-resident, box 3 taxes your global wealth on a fictitious return. It stops the day you break residency — but the Belastingdienst can challenge that date if ties remain.

**Emigratieheffing (exit tax on substantial interest).** If you own ≥5% of a BV (or equivalent), departure is treated as a deemed sale for capital gains purposes. You can **defer** the liability with the right structure (conservatoire assessment, bank guarantee), but you cannot escape it without triggering penalties.

**Woonplaatsverklaring van Emigratie.** The formal statement of emigration. Without it, the Belastingdienst presumes continued residency. With it, date certainty on the break.

**Practical sequencing we see work:**
- File intent to emigrate with the Belastingdienst 4 weeks before departure
- Obtain bank guarantee for emigratieheffing deferral (arrange in parallel)
- Break formal ties same week you fly: deregister from BRP, notify healthcare insurer, close non-essential local bank accounts
- File partial-year box 3 up to departure date with the following spring's return

**Budget:** EUR 2,000–5,000 in Dutch-side professional fees for clean emigration. Worth every euro.

## Germany — the exit-tax trap

Germany's Wegzugsteuer applies to substantial holdings (≥1% of a corporation). Unlike the Dutch case, the German rules for post-EU-exit destinations tightened in 2022 — Paraguay, not being in the EU/EEA, triggers immediate payment rather than deferral.

**What this means in practice:**
- If your main assets are a salaried role + portfolio of publicly-traded shares: clean exit, minimal tax impact.
- If you own ≥1% of a German GmbH or AG: Wegzugsteuer applies on the deemed capital gain. Deferral options are narrower than the Dutch case.
- If you hold investment assets in a German private bank: the bank's departure-customer process can take 6–8 weeks.

**Practical sequencing:** engage a German tax advisor (Steuerberater) 3–6 months before departure; consider intermediate EU jurisdiction if GmbH structure is large enough to warrant it; close tax year cleanly — emigration triggers a short-year return.

**Budget:** EUR 3,000–8,000 for tax advisor + any required valuations.

## Belgium — middle complexity

Below Germany's, above France's. Three notes:

- **Belgian securities transaction tax (TOB)** continues to apply to Belgian-resident investors for a wash-out period after departure. Not enormous but worth planning.
- **Tax on exit-year capital gains** on certain structures (SPA, SPRL equivalent stakes).
- **Registration at the commune** — Belgian emigration is declared at the municipal level, not just national tax authority.

**Budget:** EUR 1,500–3,000 Belgian-side.

## UK — the deemed-domicile question

For UK investors, the question isn't residency — you can break UK residency cleanly via the Statutory Residence Test (SRT). The question is **domicile**, a common-law concept that's stickier than tax residency.

- You can be non-UK-resident but still UK-domiciled for IHT purposes
- Domicile changes require demonstrated intent + severed ties + new domicile of choice (which Paraguay supports)
- Most investors get non-residency in the first full tax year after departure; domicile change takes 3–5 years of consistent evidence

**Budget:** GBP 2,000–6,000 UK tax advisor fees.

## Spain — simpler than most

Spain's regime is straightforward for most profiles: declare emigration to the Agencia Tributaria, last full-year Spanish return for the calendar year of departure, deregister at the Padrón. Most Spanish investors exit cleanly in 3–4 months.

**Budget:** EUR 800–2,000 for asesor fiscal fees.

## Italy — paperwork burden without the tax sting

Italy's regime for departing residents is mostly about filing. No exit tax on most profiles, but the AIRE registration (Italians abroad) + last-year return + tax-year close can take 6 months of back-and-forth.

**Budget:** EUR 1,000–3,000 for commercialista fees.
