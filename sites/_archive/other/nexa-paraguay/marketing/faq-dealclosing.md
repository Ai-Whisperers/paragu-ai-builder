## Tax Questions (The Core)

### Q1: "Is the 10% tax rate guaranteed?"

**Short Answer:** Yes, it's the law (Ley 6380, since 2020).

**Full Answer:**
The 10% IRE (Impuesto a la Renta Empresarial) is the current corporate income tax rate under Paraguayan law (Ley 6380, effective since 2020). This isn't a "special rate" or "loophole" — it's the standard corporate rate. The territorial system (no tax on foreign income) has been policy since 2014. Both have remained stable through multiple administrations. While no tax law is permanent, the stability record is strong, and there's no current political movement to change it. We structure your setup to remain compliant under whatever rules apply.

---

### Q2: "Will my home country still tax me?"

**Short Answer:** Once you properly relocate, typically no.

**Full Answer:**
This depends on your tax residency. Once you formally emigrate and cut ties (for Netherlands: woonplaatsverklaring; for Belgium: tax emigration declaration; for Germany: Abmeldung), your home country generally loses the right to tax your worldwide income. Paraguay's territorial system then applies: only income generated *within* Paraguay is taxed at 10%. Your foreign income (dividends, royalties, rental income, consulting fees from non-Paraguayan sources) is taxed at 0%. We coordinate with your home-country tax advisors to ensure the transition is clean.

---

### Q3: "What about the Dutch exit tax / box 3?"

**Short Answer:** It can often be deferred, not avoided.

**Full Answer:**
Dutch exit tax (exitheffing) on "aanmerkelijk belang" (substantial interest, typically >5% shareholding) is triggered when you renounce Dutch tax residency. However, it can often be **deferred** rather than paid immediately — especially if you relocate to another EU/EEA country (Paraguay is not in the EEA, but there are structuring options). Box 3 ends once you formally exit — but the key is timing. We coordinate with Dutch tax counsel to structure your exit so you don't get double-taxed in a transition year. You should involve your Dutch accountant from the start. We can connect you with specialists.

### Q4: "How does this compare to Portugal's old NHR?"

**Short Answer:** Paraguay is better now. Portugal's NHR is dead.

**Full Answer:**
Portugal's Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) program — which offered 20% flat tax — is essentially dead as of 2024-2025. Current rates are 20-48%. Paraguay offers 10% IRE + territorial system (no tax on foreign income) + no investment requirement + no days requirement. The math is simple: for income over €60K/year, Paraguay wins. If you were counting on Portugal, it's time to pivot.

---

### Q5: "Is Paraguay on the EU tax blacklist?"

**Short Answer:** No. It's not blacklisted.

**Full Answer:**
Paraguay is **not** on the EU's list of non-cooperative tax jurisdictions (the "blacklist"). It has exchange of information agreements and participates in CRS (Common Reporting Standard). This means: legitimate tax planning, not evasion. Your home-country tax authority will know about your Paraguayan account (just as they'd know about any foreign account), but that doesn't trigger taxation — your tax residency does.

### Q11: "What about banking? I've heard it's difficult."

**Full Answer:**
Banking is the hardest part of any Paraguayan relocation — and where most DIY attempts fail. We have relationships with Banco do Brasil, Banco Continental, and Itau. We **introduce you** to the bank, provide supporting documentation, and in most cases, accounts are opened during your 2-day trip. ### Q12: "Can I keep my current bank account in NL/BE/DE?"

**Full Answer:**
This depends on the bank. Some Dutch banks (ING, ABN) have closed accounts for residents who relocate to non-EU countries. Others (Rabobank, some regional banks) are more flexible. Our advice: keep your home-country bank account open for 6-12 months after relocation to ensure clean tax transition. After that, you can likely close it. We recommend opening your Paraguayan account first, then gradually transitioning.

## Lifestyle Questions

### Q13: "Is it safe?"

**Full Answer:**
Asunción is safer than many assume. The expat neighborhoods (Manorá, Los Laureles, gated communities near Shopping del Sol) are comparable to European suburban crime rates. Clients walk, go to restaurants, send kids to schools — without issues. Like any city, there are areas to avoid. We provide neighborhood guidance as part of the onboarding. Overall: 250+ clients have relocated successfully, and we'd know if there were systemic safety issues.

### Q15: "Can I work remotely for my current employer?"

**Full Answer:**
Yes, with caveats. As a resident, you can work for any employer (Paraguayan or foreign) or be self-employed. However, if you're working remotely for a Dutch/Belgian/German employer while physically in Paraguay, there may be payroll tax implications for your employer. Most clients on "remote work" arrangements either:
1. Have their employer set up a local payroll in Paraguay (we can advise)
2. Structure as self-employed / freelance
3. Are already business owners who invoice from their Paraguayan company

The specifics depend on your employment situation. Let's discuss during your consultation.
