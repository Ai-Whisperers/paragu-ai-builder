**Updated:** April 2026

> **Core Principle:** Lead with tax savings. Residency is the mechanism, not the message. Every objection should connect back to the 10% IRE and territorial system.

## Objection Category: FEAR

### Objection: "What if I change my mind / it's not for me?"

**Response:**
> "Two points: First, residency doesn't lock you in. You can maintain it with a visit every 3 years. It's not like renouncing your citizenship. Second, the tax savings continue as long as you hold the residency — so even if you spend part of the year elsewhere, you're still benefiting from the territorial system. And if you truly decide it's not for you after 12 months, there's no penalty — you simply don't renew."

Even in worst-case scenarios, you'd have years of warning. The savings you've collected in the meantime are yours."

## Objection Category: LEGAL / TAX

### Objection: "I need my own lawyer / accountant"

**Response:**
> "Absolutely — and we'd encourage it for your home-country tax situation. We don't replace your Dutch/Belgian/German tax advisor; we coordinate with them. Our Paraguayan team handles the local setup (residency, company, banking), and your home-country advisor handles the exit tax and ongoing compliance. We've worked with most major NL/BE tax firms and know how to structure things to minimize friction."

---

### Objection: "I don't understand the tax system / Is it too good to be true?"

**Response:**
> "I get the skepticism — if it sounds too good to be true, it usually is. Here's the reality: Paraguay's 10% IRE (Impuesto a la Renta Empresarial) is law, passed in 2019 (Ley 6380), effective since 2020. It's not a loophole — it's the standard corporate rate. The territorial system (no tax on foreign income) has been policy since 2014. This isn't a "tax haven" in the illegal sense — it's a legitimate tax planning destination that's not on the EU blacklist. The question isn't whether it's real — it's whether it's right for your specific situation. That's what the consultation is for."

---

### Objection: "What about CRS / information sharing?"

**Response:**
> "Paraguay participates in CRS (Common Reporting Standard), so financial institutions do report account information. However — and this is key — CRS is about *reporting*, not *taxation*. Having a Paraguayan account doesn't trigger home-country taxes. What matters is your tax residency. Once you properly relocate and cut ties (for NL: woonplaatsverklaring; for BE: tax emigration), your home country has no basis to tax your worldwide income. Paraguay's territorial system then applies: only income generated in Paraguay is taxed at 10%."

## Objection Category: EXIT TAX (NL-SPECIFIC)

### Objection: "What about Dutch exit tax?"

**Response:**
> "This is where structure matters. Dutch exit tax on "aanmerkelijk belang" (substantial interest, typically >5% shareholding) can often be deferred rather than avoided — especially if you relocate to an EU/EEA country (which Paraguay is not, but there are structuring options). We work with Dutch tax counsel who specialize in this. The key is timing: we coordinate with your Dutch advisor to structure the exit to minimize or defer the exit tax hit. It's not about avoiding — it's about legitimate deferral. Your Dutch accountant should be involved from day one, and we're happy to coordinate with them."

---

### Objection: "Box 3 after emigration"

**Response:**
> "Once you properly emigrate and cut tax ties (woonplaatsverklaring), box 3 no longer applies — you're no longer a Dutch tax resident. The issue is the timing: you can't be in both systems simultaneously. We coordinate with your Dutch advisor to ensure you don't get double-taxed in a transition year. For most clients, the cleanest approach is: final Dutch tax return, then file for tax residency change, then Paraguayan setup. We can manage this with your Dutch accountant."

---

## Objection Category: BELGIUM-SPECIFIC

### Objection: "Belgian exit tax"

**Response:**
> "Belgium also has exit tax provisions (taxatie bij vertrek). The structure is similar to the Dutch exit tax — it's a deferral mechanism, not permanent avoidance. Paraguay isn't in the EU, which actually provides some planning opportunities. We work with Belgian tax advisors who understand the Belgian exit tax rules. The key is to ensure your exit is clean and documented. Would you like us to connect you with a Belgian tax specialist we've worked with?"
