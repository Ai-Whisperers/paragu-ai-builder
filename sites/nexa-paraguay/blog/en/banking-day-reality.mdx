The Paraguayan bank appointment is the single step in our process that investors worry about most. Forums are full of horror stories — "I spent six weeks being passed between branches" or "The compliance officer rejected me without explanation." Those stories are real, but they're almost entirely the result of walking in cold, without a relationship, without prepared documentation, without a file already reviewed.

Here's what a coordinated bank appointment actually looks like when the paperwork is in order.

## Before the day

By the time you walk into the bank, three things are already true:

1. **The bank has seen your file.** We send it 10 days ahead through a named relationship contact — not a generic teller. That person has already flagged any compliance issues and come back with questions. When you arrive, you're not an unknown.
2. **Your documents are pre-approved in format.** Apostille, translations, income source evidence — the bank's compliance desk has green-lit the file form. You walk in with a sheet that says "everything checked."
3. **The specific KYC profile is agreed.** Paraguay's 2023 AML update (Resolución SEPRELAD 137) tightened source-of-funds documentation. We've supplied and matched the evidence to your declared profile ahead of the meeting.

This pre-work is the reason our bank appointments close in 60–90 minutes on the day. Without it, the same paperwork takes 4–6 weeks of back-and-forth.

## What the 60–90 minutes look like

**Minutes 0–10:** welcome, passport verification, file review with the named officer. You sign the KYC declaration.

**Minutes 10–30:** account opening forms. Pick account type (current + savings, USD + PYG multi-currency is standard). Sign the contracts. Initial deposit confirmation.

**Minutes 30–60:** online banking enrollment. They hand you a physical token (yes, still a physical device at most Paraguayan banks in 2026), walk you through first-login, set your initial PIN. You get card-printing scheduled or pick up a provisional card if available.

**Minutes 60–90:** tax registration bridge. If we're opening on the same day as your Migraciones appointment, the bank needs a RUC (tax ID) to fully activate — we coordinate with SET to get provisional RUC issuance in parallel. Cards, tokens, mobile app setup.

That's the day. You leave with an account number, an activation timeline, and a named contact at the bank.

## What activates on the day vs what takes time

**Same day:**
- Account number issued
- Initial USD deposit credited
- Online banking token enrolled
- Mobile app linked

**3–7 business days after:**
- First wire transfer clears (incoming from your home country)
- International debit card arrives

**2–3 weeks after:**
- Local debit card mailed
- Automatic bill-pay / direct-debit enabled
- Full credit-card issuance (separate application)

The physical card delay is the one thing that catches people. Plan cash or international card access for the first 2 weeks in Paraguay.

## The documentation that actually matters

Most rejections we see among direct walk-ins are over source-of-funds. Paraguayan banks in 2026 want:

1. Passport + second government ID from your home country
2. Proof of address (utility bill within 90 days)
3. Apostilled certificate of Paraguayan residency application (we provide — this is why the bank usually happens same-day as Migraciones)
4. Income evidence — for employed: last 3 payslips + employer letter. For business owners: last 2 years of company accounts + tax returns. For investors: broker statements + source of wealth declaration.
5. Bank reference letter from your home-country bank — this is the document that takes longest to obtain in Europe. Start early.

The source-of-wealth narrative matters more than total amount. USD 50K from a documented 5-year consulting career clears faster than USD 500K "from investments" without paper trails.

## What we do when banks say no

It happens. Most common reasons: PEP classification (politically exposed person) via someone in your family tree; unexplained deposits in your home-country account history; residency visa from a jurisdiction on FATF grey list; income source that the bank's AML profile doesn't cover.

When a bank declines, we: get written reasons (sometimes verbally), route the file to a bank whose profile accepts it, package differently if needed, escalate to the bank's relationship director if the decline looks procedural rather than substantive.

In 3 years of running Paraguayan setups, zero of our prepared clients have ended the program without an active bank account. The process takes longer for some profiles — it doesn't fail.
