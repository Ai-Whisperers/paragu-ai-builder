Every European investor who walks into our first call has already heard the pitch for the other two. Paraguay, Uruguay, and Panama are the three Latin American jurisdictions that dominate relocation conversations — and for good reason. Each has a territorial tax angle, each is politically stable, and each has lived through the post-2020 wave of European emigration.

This is the honest side-by-side. We work in Paraguay, but we've sent clients to Uruguay and Panama when it was the right call for them. Here's how the three actually compare in 2026.

## The 30-second summary

| | Paraguay | Uruguay | Panama |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate tax | 10% (IRE) | 25% (IRAE) | 25% (capped by territorial rules) |
| Tax on foreign income | **0% (territorial)** | 0–12% (territorial with exceptions) | 0% (territorial) |
| Residency investment | **None required** | USD 525,000 real estate (FRV) | USD 200,000 real estate (QIP) |
| Timeline to residency | 8–12 weeks | 12–18 months | 4–6 months |
| Cost of living vs Berlin | **~50% lower** | ~15–25% lower | ~30% lower |
| EU tax-haven list | Not listed | Not listed | Not listed |
| Banking difficulty | Moderate | High | High (post-2020) |
| Practical language | Spanish + Guaraní | Spanish | Spanish + English |

If you only read one row: **Paraguay doesn't require an investment to get residency.** That's the single biggest structural difference between the three. Uruguay and Panama are "invest to reside" jurisdictions with meaningful capital minimums. Paraguay is "establish a real link and apply."

## Paraguay — the under-priced option

**The pitch:** residency without required investment, 10% corporate tax on locally-sourced income, 0% on foreign income, and the lowest cost of living of the three.

**Who it fits:**
- Remote workers, consultants, and freelancers with mostly foreign-source income
- Business owners wanting a real operating base (not a paper one) in Mercosur
- Investors who want optionality — residency first, investment decision separately
- Agribusiness: Paraguay is the 4th-largest soy exporter globally, land is transactable

**Where it breaks down:**
- Infrastructure is developing — parts of the country have intermittent utilities
- Banking requires real documentation and takes 4–8 weeks (not 24 hours)
- Spanish is the operating language; English is limited outside Asunción's professional district
- No bilateral tax treaty with Netherlands, Germany, or Belgium (this is almost never a problem in practice because of the territorial system, but it surprises people)

**Typical cost to get fully set up:** EUR 3,000–7,000 for residency + company + bank account through a professional team. That's the entire setup, not a retainer.

## Uruguay — the premium option

**The pitch:** Europe-grade rule of law, Montevideo as a livable-but-small European-style capital, proximity to Buenos Aires and São Paulo.

**Who it fits:**
- Families prioritizing schools and healthcare over tax optimization
- Investors committing to one location for a decade+
- Anyone with USD 500K+ liquid to deploy into real estate and who wants that asset to double as the residency ticket

**Where it breaks down:**
- Cost. Montevideo isn't cheap. Rent, food, services, and imports are materially higher than Buenos Aires or Asunción.
- Speed. Bureaucracy is thorough. 12–18 months to full residency is normal, not a worst case.
- Tax. The 25% IRAE corporate rate is higher than people assume. Foreign income is mostly exempt but there are carve-outs that bite — especially on dividends from certain treaty countries.
- Banking is harder than Paraguay for non-residents.

**Typical cost to get fully set up:** USD 8,000–15,000 in professional fees + USD 525,000 qualifying real estate investment (if using the FRV investor route) or demonstrated local income equivalent.

## Panama — the legacy option

**The pitch:** dollar-based economy, the Canal, historical expat community, English widely spoken in professional settings.

**Who it fits:**
- US citizens who want to stay in USD and don't want to convert thinking
- Investors using the Friendly Nations Visa (now requires USD 200K real estate or USD 5K deposit + demonstrated economic ties)
- Regional headquarters for Latin America operations

**Where it breaks down:**
- The 2021 Friendly Nations Visa reform materially raised the bar. It used to be trivially cheap for 50 nationalities; now it requires real investment.
- Banking post-2020 is harder than Paraguay for European passports. US banks pulled back from Panama correspondents; EU banks followed.
- Panama City is a big city with big-city problems — traffic, cost of living, and a tax-haven reputation that still follows certain industries even though Panama isn't on the current EU list.
- Cost of living is mid-range: cheaper than Madrid, pricier than Asunción.

**Typical cost to get fully set up:** USD 10,000–20,000 in professional fees + USD 200,000 investment for the Qualified Investor track or USD 5K deposit + documented economic ties for Friendly Nations.

## How we actually help investors pick

The decision is almost never about tax rates. When it is, Paraguay wins cleanly — 10% corporate + 0% foreign + no minimum investment is mathematically the lowest.

The decision is usually about fit:

- If you want a **premium, slow, stable** base and you have the capital — Uruguay.
- If you're American and want to stay **dollar-denominated with English services** — Panama.
- If you want to **minimize friction, preserve capital, and move fast** — Paraguay.

We're biased toward Paraguay because we live here, we operate here, and we've seen too many European families who overpaid for Uruguay residency and then moved to Asunción six months later for the cost of living. But we're honest about the trade. If Uruguay is the right call for you, we'll tell you.
