We see this conversation about twice a month:

> **Prospect:** "I got Paraguayan residency through an agency last year. I'm paying 10% now, right?"
> **Us:** "Are you spending more than 6 months in Paraguay?"
> **Prospect:** "Not really. I travel a lot."
> **Us:** "Where does your home-country tax authority think you live?"

Long pause.

The distinction between **legal residency** (the paperwork) and **tax residency** (the fiscal concept) is the single point where investors most often get blindsided. This post is the 5-minute version.

## The two concepts

**Legal residency** is a migration status. It's what the cédula and the Paraguayan residency resolution give you. It means: you can live in Paraguay indefinitely, open a bank account, incorporate a company, sign a lease, buy property, and have a Paraguayan government-issued ID.

It does **not** automatically mean: Paraguay considers you its tax resident; your home country considers you non-resident; your worldwide income flows into the 10% IRE system.

**Tax residency** is determined by factual tests, not paperwork. Typical tests include physical presence (most commonly 183+ days in the jurisdiction), centre of vital interests (family, home, business), habitual abode, and nationality (as a tiebreaker).

You can hold Paraguayan legal residency while being tax-resident in the Netherlands. Or vice versa. The cédula doesn't move the tax needle on its own.

## The 183-day rule is the start, not the end

Most jurisdictions (Paraguay included) use 183 days in the country as the primary presence test for tax residency. Spend >183 days in Paraguay in a calendar year, and Paraguay considers you tax resident for that year.

But it's not the only test. Most treaty countries layer additional criteria:

- **Netherlands:** 183 days is decisive for becoming NL-resident, but breaking residency requires also severing ties (no rented home waiting, no kids in Dutch school, no dominant commercial activity in NL).
- **Germany:** similar layered test. German Wegzugsteuer + continued ownership of a German Wohnung can keep you resident even with 300 days abroad.
- **UK:** the Statutory Residence Test (SRT) is a structured multi-factor test, not a simple day count.
- **Spain:** 183 days is decisive *and* centre-of-economic-interests can override it.

**Practical consequence:** if you get Paraguayan residency but stay >183 days in your home country, your home country still taxes you worldwide. Paraguay's 10% isn't active for you yet.

## The worst-case scenario: dual residency

Worse than being resident in your home country is being resident **in both**. Dual tax residency triggers filing obligations in both jurisdictions, potential double taxation (mitigated by treaties — but Paraguay has few treaties), and tiebreaker clauses in the treaty (if one exists) that usually land you back where you started.

Paraguay has no bilateral tax treaty with Netherlands, Germany, Belgium, UK, or France. The territorial system means this is rarely painful in practice — Paraguay only taxes Paraguayan-source income, so even if you're theoretically resident, there's nothing for Paraguay to double-tax. But it does mean you can't use treaty tiebreakers to force the home country to release you.

The home country releases you when you demonstrably live somewhere else. That demonstration is 183+ days plus factual ties — a lease, a bank account used as primary, kids in local school, local doctor — all of which require you to actually be in Paraguay.

## What this means in planning

Three types of profile, three recommendations:

**1. "I want Paraguayan residency but I'll mostly stay in Europe"** — Get the cédula for optionality. It's useful for Mercosur travel, business setup, and future-optionality. But understand that you're not getting the 10% tax benefit until you actually move. The residency is legal; the tax savings require presence.

**2. "I want to move to Paraguay properly and be Paraguayan tax resident"** — Plan for the 183-day physical-presence threshold in your first full year. Break factual ties with the home country cleanly. Budget for the home-country exit paperwork in parallel.

**3. "I want to split time and be resident nowhere"** — Almost always the wrong goal. Statelessness-for-tax-purposes is a setup most tax authorities will collapse back to one jurisdiction the moment they audit. The cleanest path to low overall tax is to be firmly tax resident in one low-tax jurisdiction, not no jurisdiction.

## The decision tree

Before committing to a program, three questions:

1. **Will you actually spend 183+ days in Paraguay in the first full calendar year after residency?** If yes: Paraguayan tax residency kicks in cleanly. If no: you're getting legal residency only.

2. **Is your home country a treaty country with Paraguay?** For the EU: mostly no. This works in Paraguay's favour (no tiebreaker to fight over) but means you need stronger factual evidence of the move.

3. **Can you break factual ties with the home country in one move?** Yes: plan a clean departure date, 6–12 months of preparation, one clean move. No (family, property, business): plan a multi-step exit over 18–24 months.
