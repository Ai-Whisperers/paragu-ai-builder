Paraguay's reputation as a low-tax jurisdiction is earned. The headline numbers — 10% corporate income tax (IRE), 0% on foreign-source income, no wealth tax, no capital gains tax on most personal asset sales — are real, written into law, and have been stable since the 2019 tax reform (Ley 6380).

But the details matter. We've turned down prospects whose structure didn't fit, and we've restructured plans for investors who thought they qualified when they didn't. This post is the decision tree we actually run through on every consultation.

## The three rules in one sentence each

1. **Corporate tax (IRE) is a flat 10%** on net profit derived from Paraguayan sources.
2. **Foreign-source income is not taxed in Paraguay** — this is the territorial principle, hard-coded in Art. 73 of Ley 6380.
3. **Personal income (IRP)** is 8% or 10% depending on bracket, applied only to Paraguayan-source earnings above the minimum threshold.

Everything else is application of these three rules to your specific structure.

## Rule 1: "Paraguayan source" — the definition that matters

The territorial system only works if you know which side of the source line your income sits on. Paraguay defines Paraguayan-source income (roughly) as:

- Services rendered **inside** Paraguay
- Sale of goods located **inside** Paraguay at time of sale
- Rental of assets located in Paraguay
- Dividends from Paraguayan companies

Conversely, **foreign-source** is:
- Services rendered outside Paraguay (even by a Paraguayan resident)
- Sales concluded outside Paraguay
- Investment income from foreign assets (dividends, interest, capital gains on foreign securities)
- Rental from real estate located outside Paraguay

**Edge cases that trip people up:**

- A remote consultant living in Asunción invoicing a Dutch client for work done from her laptop in Paraguay: the service is rendered **in Paraguay**, so that revenue is Paraguayan-source. It's taxable at 10% IRE if billed through a Paraguayan company.
- A Paraguayan-resident investor receiving dividends from a German holding company: **foreign-source.** Not taxed in Paraguay. Still potentially taxed in Germany depending on your German tax situation.
- Rent from a Madrid apartment owned personally by a Paraguayan resident: **foreign-source.** Not taxed in Paraguay.

The pattern: look at **where the economic activity occurs**, not where the person lives.

## Rule 2: The 10% IRE rate applies to net profit, not revenue

IRE is calculated on **net profit** — revenue minus deductible expenses — for Paraguayan-source activity. Real operating costs are deductible: salaries, rent, professional fees, depreciation, financial costs, and most services-in. What that means in practice:

A consultant billing USD 120,000 of Paraguayan-source revenue with USD 30,000 of legitimate deductible expenses has USD 90,000 of taxable profit. IRE is USD 9,000. Effective rate: 7.5%.

The only structures where the 10% headline is misleading:
- Pure holding companies with no operating expenses
- Shell structures with no real economic activity (which risk being re-characterized anyway)

If you're running a real business, the effective rate is usually below 10%.

## Rule 3: VAT (IVA) is separate and unavoidable

Paraguayan VAT is 10% standard rate (5% for specific goods like basic foodstuffs, certain medicines, and some real estate). It applies to nearly all sales and services inside Paraguay.

This is the one number that often surprises investors who focus only on IRE. You pay VAT on imports, on professional services, on rent (sometimes), on vehicle purchases. It's recoverable if you're IVA-registered and have matching input credits.

**In practice:** a new Paraguayan company incorporated under Nexa's Business program carries an ongoing IVA obligation from day one. This is why real accounting matters — most of the "effective tax rate" calculation lives in managing IVA credits cleanly.

## The qualifier questions we run on every consult

Before we commit to a structure, we ask:

1. **Where does your income actually originate?** If >70% is foreign-source, the territorial system is a clean win and Paraguay is probably right. If >70% is Paraguayan-source, we're optimizing within IRE rather than escaping it.
2. **Do you have a tie that the Paraguayan tax authority would care about?** Local spouse, local kids in school, local office with employees, local bank loans — these establish presence.
3. **What's your current home-country tax position?** A Dutch resident with a Dutch BV and box 3 wealth needs a different plan than a German remote worker with no assets. The NL exit tax (emigratieheffing) and box 3 rules don't disappear when you land in Paraguay — they have to be actively unwound.
4. **Are you going to spend >183 days in Paraguay annually?** This is the rough residency test. Less than that and your Paraguayan residency won't hold up as your primary tax residence in most treaty-based jurisdictions.

## Who Paraguay's tax system **does not** work for

Being honest about this:

- **US citizens.** The US taxes on citizenship, not residency. Paraguay's 10% doesn't save you anything federally until you renounce — and renunciation is a separate multi-year project.
- **Investors with >50% Paraguayan-source income looking to avoid tax.** The territorial system taxes local activity. If you want to run a Paraguayan business tax-free, you can't.
- **Structures where the "real" activity is in Paraguay but billing flows through offshore entities.** The transfer pricing rules (BEPS-aligned) and substance requirements will eventually catch up. Paraguay is not an aggressive tax-haven jurisdiction; it's a clean, stable, low-rate jurisdiction.
