Most relocation agencies pitch "one coordinated process" and leave the timeline as a vague promise. We publish our week-by-week because when you're about to move countries, you want to know exactly what's happening on week five.

Here's what the Nexa Business program — residency + company + bank account — actually looks like, week by week.

## The one-line summary

**Week 1:** Consultation and diagnostic. **Weeks 2–5:** Document preparation. **Week 6:** Your trip — one coordinated operational day in Asunción. **Weeks 7–9:** Company incorporation and bank opening. **Weeks 10–12:** Residency approval, cédula issuance, delivery.

Total: 8–12 weeks realistic. 8 is fast-track. 12 is standard when government issuance takes the upper end.

## Week 1 — Initial consultation + diagnostic

**What we do:**
- 30-minute free intro call
- Diagnose fit (is Paraguay actually right for you, or is Uruguay/Panama a better call?)
- Pick the program: Base, Business, Investor, Land
- If you proceed: send the engagement letter and the document checklist

**What you do:**
- One call. Maybe one follow-up email.
- Sign the engagement if you proceed.
- Pay the initial invoice (we don't start without it — this keeps us honest about who we take on).

**What can slip:** nothing material. This is a decision week.

## Weeks 2–3 — Document preparation (your side)

**What we do:**
- Send you a jurisdiction-specific document checklist (Netherlands ≠ Germany ≠ Belgium ≠ Spain — the apostille chains are different)
- Schedule a 45-min prep call to walk through the checklist in detail
- Stay on call for questions

**What you do:**
- Collect: passport, birth certificate, police clearance, marriage certificate (if applicable), bank reference letter
- Get them apostilled in your home country (this is the step that slips timelines most often — budget 10 business days)
- Ship us scans as each one comes in

**What can slip:** your home country's apostille process. The Netherlands is ~5 business days. Germany varies by Bundesland. Italy can take 4 weeks in some regions. If your birth certificate has to be re-issued first (common for people born abroad), add another 2 weeks.

## Weeks 4–5 — Document validation (our side)

**What we do:**
- Paraguayan lawyer reviews every document. Validates format, translations, and sufficiency.
- If anything is wrong, we tell you **now** — not when you're sitting in the Migraciones office.
- We prepare the migration file in parallel: all the Paraguayan-side forms, cover letters, and applications.
- We book your appointments at Migraciones, Identificaciones, and the bank. All on one day.

**What you do:**
- Mostly wait. Answer the occasional follow-up email.
- Book your flight once we confirm the operational day.

**What can slip:** if a document is rejected for form, we loop back to step 2–3 and re-issue. This is why we validate before you travel — a rejection here is a 2-week delay, a rejection after you've flown is a re-flight.

## Week 6 — The operational day

This is the trip. One day in Asunción. Everything scheduled back-to-back.

**Typical day (real client schedule, anonymized):**
- 07:30: Pickup from your hotel, breakfast briefing
- 08:30: Migraciones — residency application filed, biometrics taken
- 10:30: Identificaciones — cédula application, photo, fingerprinting
- 12:30: Lunch at a restaurant in Villa Morra (you've been awake since 6, this is non-negotiable)
- 14:00: Bank appointment — account opening, initial deposit
- 15:30: Notary — company incorporation documents signed (if on Business program)
- 17:00: Wrap-up. You're free. Your Paraguayan paperwork is in motion.

**What you do:** show up. Bring your passport and a few specific documents we'll have listed. Wear something you'd wear to a European bank meeting.

**What can slip:** almost nothing — this is the most tightly coordinated day of the program. The rare slip is the bank introducing a new compliance step mid-meeting; we have a one-day buffer built in.

## Weeks 7–9 — Company incorporation + bank activation

**What we do:**
- File company incorporation with the Paraguayan registry
- Obtain the RUC (tax ID) for the new company
- Activate the bank account (deposit clears, online banking enabled)
- Register you with the tax authority (SET) as a new taxpayer

**What you do:**
- Transfer the initial capitalization to your new bank account (you can do this from Europe; we provide the wire instructions)
- Respond to any bank KYC follow-ups (rare but happens)

**What can slip:** bank activation. Paraguayan banks have tightened KYC since 2023; some banks take 3 weeks to fully activate an account. We work with multiple banks precisely so we can route you to the fastest one for your profile.

## Weeks 10–12 — Residency approval + cédula + handoff

**What we do:**
- Follow the residency file through Migraciones. They issue the resolution (residency approval) 4–6 weeks after your operational day.
- Once approved, we go to Identificaciones and pick up your cédula.
- Courier the physical cédula to you (EU delivery is 3–5 business days via DHL)
- Deliver the final file: apostilled cédula copy, company documents, bank credentials, tax registration, and a 30-minute wrap-up call.

**What you do:**
- Wait. Maybe plan your next trip.

**What can slip:** Migraciones' internal queue. We've seen resolutions issue in 4 weeks, we've seen them take 7. When they slip, we slip — there's no pay-to-accelerate channel (and we wouldn't use one if there were).

## What's not in this timeline

Deliberately out of scope for the 8–12 weeks:
- **Renting or buying real estate.** We can coordinate tours during your operational-day visit but we don't rush this.
- **Moving your family.** We handle the lead applicant; family members are a separate track (still within the program but can run parallel).
- **Tax optimization of your European exit.** Handled with your home-country tax advisor, not us.
- **Enrolling kids in Paraguayan schools.** We provide a list of international schools; enrollment is your call.

## The honest caveat

The 8–12 weeks assumes your documents come through cleanly. If your home country's apostille office has a backlog, if your birth certificate needs to be re-issued, if your German bank takes 3 weeks to produce a bank reference letter — add that time. The Paraguayan side of the process is tightly controlled. The home-country side is where most slippage happens.

**The best mitigation:** start the document collection the day you engage. We've had clients finish the full program in 8 weeks precisely because they treated week 1's checklist as a hard list, not a reference.
